The cost of leaving it as-is

The reporting gap doesn't show up on an invoice. It shows up at the bid.

Tonnages, stream weights and proof-of-service sit in paper logs, a spreadsheet one person understands, and message threads. None of it is wrong — it's just unprovable when it matters. Three places that quietly costs you:

Lost & defended tenders

A competitor hands over a clean, verified report at tender. You hand over a promise. The work was comparable; the proof wasn't — and the bid went the other way.

  • No defensible number at the point of decision
  • Renewals slip when the client can't see the value

Weeks of manual admin

Compiling one client report takes weeks of skilled time — chasing logs, rebuilding spreadsheets, formatting a PDF. By the time it lands, the moment to impress has passed.

  • Senior people doing data entry, not delivery
  • The report arrives after it could have changed anything

Compliance exposure

Simpler Recycling 2025, BREEAM and ISO 14001 reporting all assume a traceable trail. When the trail is manual, an audit or a client request becomes a fire drill.

  • No timestamped, traceable record to fall back on
  • Disputes turn into your word against theirs

The point isn't a scary number — it's that the cost is invisible until a single won-or-lost contract makes it very visible. Against that, the question isn't "what does this software cost?" It's "what does one lost tender cost?"
