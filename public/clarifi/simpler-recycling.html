What changed

One set of rules. Separate streams.

Under England's Simpler Recycling reforms, workplaces must separate their waste into consistent streams rather than mixing recyclables into general waste — arranging collection of dry recyclables (plastic, metal, glass, paper and card) and food waste separately from residual general waste. The goal is a single, consistent system across the country, and an end to the patchwork of local rules.

For multi-site and contract operators, the operational reality is the hard part: more streams, more segregation points, and a client who now expects to see the numbers — not just be told the bins were emptied.
Who it affects & when

Two deadlines — know which one is yours

10+ employees: separate dry-recycling and food-waste collection is required from 31 March 2025 — in force now.
<10 employees (micro-firms): the same requirements apply from 31 March 2027.

Guidance only — confirm the current rules and any exemptions for your operation at GOV.UK ("Simpler Recycling: workplace recycling in England") or with your waste regulator. This guide is not legal advice.

The action list

Four things every operator should do

1

Map your streams, per site

Document which streams each site produces and how they're segregated. Inconsistent segregation is the most common contamination — and dispute — trigger.

2

Fix the capture point

Record weights, stream types and compaction at the point of work, on a phone — not reconstructed from memory days later. Data you didn't capture, you can't report.

3

Make the evidence auditable

Timestamped, photo-backed, per-stream records turn "trust us" into "here's the proof" — for inspectors and for clients alike.

4

Report in the client's language

Diversion rates, tonnage, environmental impact — presented as a branded report, not a spreadsheet attachment three weeks later.

The trap

Complying isn't the same as proving it.

Most operators will hit the streams requirement. Far fewer can produce, on demand, the verified evidence a client or auditor asks for — which is exactly where contracts are won and lost. The reporting gap — the weeks of manual compilation between the work and the report — is where compliance effort quietly fails to become commercial advantage.
